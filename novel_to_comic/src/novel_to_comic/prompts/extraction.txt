=== TYPE CONSTRAINTS ===

- narrative_arcs[].act: INTEGER only — use 1, 2, 3. NOT "Act 1", NOT "act_1".
- narrative_arcs[].scenes: ARRAY of scene id strings — e.g. ["scene_001", "scene_002"]. NOT a single string.
- narrative_arcs[].title: SHORT string — e.g. "Setup", "Confrontation", "Resolution".
- characters[].distinguishing_features: ARRAY of strings — e.g. ["long red hair", "scar on chin"]. NOT a single string.
- characters[].baseline_affect: STRING or null. A stable, low-intensity demeanor separate from physical identity and from scene reactions.
- scenes[].source_start_quote: VERBATIM string — copy the complete opening sentence of that scene exactly as it appears in the text. Do NOT paraphrase, summarise, or stop mid-sentence.
- characters[].appearance_states, locations[].appearance_states: ARRAY of alternate-look objects. It may be empty only after completing the mandatory VISIBLE-CHANGE INVENTORY below.
- characters[].appearance_timeline, locations[].appearance_timeline: ARRAY of activations, each {state_id, at:{scene_id, beat}, transition?}, ordered by story position. It may be empty only when the corresponding state catalog is empty.
- scenes[].transient_objects: ARRAY (may be empty). See TRANSIENT OBJECTS rules below.

=== EXTRACTION RULES ===

CHARACTERS
- Include any named character who physically participates in 2+ scenes, not merely someone named in them.
- Also include every named on-screen speaking character whose testimony,
  explanation, decision, or reaction is important to understanding the story,
  even when that character appears in only one scene. A comic cannot assign
  dialogue balloons or stable visual identity to a missing speaker.
- Also include every plot-critical embodied actor even if silent and present in
  only one scene: an antagonist, creature, animal, rescuer, victim, or witness
  whose physical action is essential to the scene. A creature that emerges,
  attacks, pursues, or is struck is a Character, not a prop or background lore.
  Check the climax and each scene's decisive action against its character roster
  before returning JSON. Diary/testimony framing does not exclude those actors.
- Include only characters actually staged by the source, not anyone an adaptation could imagine showing.
- For every candidate character, supply character_participation evidence for each scene in whose roster they occur. Use exactly one classification per character/scene pair and a nonempty VERBATIM source passage. Keep each source_quote to a short, sufficient 2-8 word fragment so you can copy it directly from the supplied text; preserve its punctuation and Unicode characters, and never reconstruct, normalize, or paraphrase it. This is extraction-only evidence, not physical identity.
- direct_action: the source stages the character physically acting, speaking, or undergoing an event in the scene. Important one-scene embodied actors and speakers qualify.
- staged_recollection: the source narrates an embodied sequence in a memory, diary, dream, testimony, or flashback, with the character physically acting or undergoing the event. Retrospective tense alone is not grounds for exclusion. A narrated sickbed sequence or a diary's staged voyage qualifies even when framed by a present-day speaker.
- background_only: mentions, relationship exposition, biographical summaries, off-screen voices, unseen speakers, and people represented only in background photographs or described social-media images. A reported trip or relationship fact is not an embodied flashback just because it could be illustrated. Do not invent an absent person's appearance, action, or scene to justify eligibility.
- Exclude background-only people from Character entries and scenes[].character_ids; preserve relevant facts/dialogue in summaries. If a candidate Character is returned for classification, mark its evidence background_only so the extraction boundary can remove it and its references. A person staged elsewhere still must not enter the roster of a scene that only mentions them.
- Every scenes[].character_ids entry requires direct_action or staged_recollection evidence in that scene. Keep distinct embodied flashbacks in their own source-ordered scenes; never promote a background photo or relationship exposition into a new staged scene.
- visual_anchor = Danbooru-style comma-separated PHYSICAL IDENTITY tags ONLY. No prose, sentences, emotion, expression, mood, pose, or action. Include the character's typical recognizable anatomy, physical traits, usual clothing, and usual accessories. Exclude both transient affect ("anxious expression", "terrified", "smiling", "wild-eyed") and situational/changed state ("wet", "injured", "shivering", "covered in blood", "exhausted", "silver hands"). Any sustained, story-significant changed condition belongs in appearance_states + appearance_timeline, even when temporary or reversible; it is not limited to permanent body changes.
- baseline_affect owns any stable demeanor supported by the source, expressed at low intensity: e.g. "guarded and watchful", "habitually stern", "quietly uneasy". It is NOT part of visual_anchor. Do not record beat-level reactions here; screenplay/panel expression direction owns those. Prefer null when the source establishes no stable demeanor.
- Ground every visual_anchor tag in textual evidence from the source. Do NOT invent specific clothing items, facial features, or accessories that are not supported by the text.
- Prefer omission over fabrication. If details are sparse, use broader but evidence-supported tags instead of made-up specifics.
- Preserve deliberate ambiguity from the source text. If the text frames a character as gender-ambiguous/indeterminate, keep that ambiguity in the tags and avoid collapsing to a strongly gendered archetype unless the source explicitly does so.
- Highlight source-supported physical distinctions between recurring characters who share a scene. Never invent contrasting traits to meet a quota; sparse evidence may leave visual design decisions for the author and Cast.
- Avoid generic filler physical tags that make characters converge (e.g., "average face"). Expression and posture do not belong in visual_anchor at all; put a stable demeanor in baseline_affect and leave scene reactions to panel direction.
  WRONG: "A tall woman with short black hair who is wearing a tactical jacket."
  WRONG: "1woman, adult female, tall, short black hair, grey eyes, shivering, thin torn clothes, lips blue from cold" (situational/changed state — belongs in appearance_states)
  RIGHT: "1woman, adult female, tall, short black hair, grey eyes, jagged scar on cheek, olive skin, athletic build, dark tactical jacket, red arm patch"
- default_outfit = supplementary clothing details the anchor tags may not fully capture. e.g. "cargo pants, combat boots, dog tags around neck"
- Use age-appropriate count/gender tags for HUMAN characters only:
  - Adult human male characters MUST start with "1man, adult male" — NEVER "1boy".
  - Adult human female characters MUST start with "1woman, adult female" — NEVER "1girl".
  - Human child male characters use "1boy, child".
  - Human child female characters use "1girl, child".
- Animals and nonhuman creatures retain their source-supported species, anatomy, and appropriate count tags (for example "1cat"). Age or gender never licenses replacing nonhuman anatomy with a human identity.
- distinguishing_features: PERMANENT, state-invariant identity details true for the WHOLE story (scars present throughout, hair, marks). NEVER a feature the character acquires or loses partway through (severed hands, prosthetics, a wound, aging) — those are appearance_states, not identity. A tag like "maimed arms" here would brand her maimed in every panel, including before the injury. The base visual_anchor and distinguishing_features must describe the earliest depictable pre-change look in the character's lived chronology. If the source first mentions a later appearance and then reveals the earlier look in a diary or flashback, the earlier look is still the base. Do not put the later look in the base visual_anchor.

LOCATIONS
- One Location entry per distinct PHYSICAL PLACE. The bunker, the apartment, the alley — each gets its own entry.
- visual_anchor = the BASE state's tags (how the place looks at its first appearance). If it only ever appears one way, this is the only anchor needed and `appearance_states` may be left empty.
- An anchor MUST describe ONE coherent visible state. NEVER fuse mutually exclusive details into one bag of tags.
  WRONG: "street lamp, parked cars, stop sign, ruins, rubble, dirt, bicycle rack, moped, debris"
         (mixes pre-collapse furniture with post-collapse aftermath in one anchor — every panel will be polluted with both)
  RIGHT: default → "night, outdoors, street, sidewalk, snow, brick apartment facade, streetlamp, parked cars, stop sign"
         state "loc_001_post" → "night, outdoors, flattened dirt, rubble, distant fire, bicycle rack, moped, debris, empty space"

LOCATION STATES (locations[].appearance_states + appearance_timeline)
- Use appearance_states when ONE physical place visibly changes mid-story:
  - before/after a destructive event (apartment block standing → flattened rubble)
  - day → night within the same set
  - intact → on-fire, clean → blood-soaked, etc.
- Each state has its OWN self-contained visual_anchor (15+ tags) describing THAT state only; never bleed in details from sibling states. Set visually_significant: true (a location state exists precisely because it looks different and needs its own plate).
- State id MUST extend the parent location id, e.g. "loc_001_post", "loc_001_burning".
- If the place is visually stable across the whole story, leave both appearance_states and appearance_timeline empty and rely on the base visual_anchor.
- Record WHEN each state takes effect in appearance_timeline (see APPEARANCE TIMELINE). Downstream stages resolve the active state per panel.

INTERIOR vs EXTERIOR
- An interior (apartment bedroom, dungeon corridor) and the exterior visible from it (street outside, sky above) are NOT the same location. A character standing in a bedroom looking out a window at the street is INSIDE — the location is the bedroom; the window-frame view is a background detail of the bedroom scene.
- If a character moves from interior to exterior within a chapter, split the events into separate scenes with separate location_ids — even if the temporal gap is small. This prevents indoor/outdoor location anchors from contaminating each other's panels.
- A flashback or cold-open that depicts a different physical location (or a clearly different time) is ALSO a separate scene. Do NOT bundle a remembered interior into the "now" exterior scene.

APPEARANCE TIMELINE (characters[] and locations[] — how a look changes over the story)
- Before writing any JSON, silently complete a VISIBLE-CHANGE INVENTORY for every character and location. Search the entire source for explicit contrasts between earlier and later looks or sustained conditions. Do this even when the source is framed, non-linear, retrospective, or embeds testimony, dreams, letters, or diaries.
- Inventory all story-significant, visually depictable intervals, including:
  - bodily transformation, injury, disability, healing, restoration, aging, changed hair or skin, and supernatural form
  - fever, delirium, unconsciousness, recovery, possession, intoxication, or another sustained condition with a distinct state-specific demeanor
  - disguise, uniform, major wardrobe era, imprisonment, contamination, blood, burns, or weather exposure when it persists for a meaningful visual sequence rather than one instantaneous reaction
  - a location becoming flooded, drained, raised, sunken, intact, ruined, burning, blood-soaked, overgrown, or changing between story-significant lighting/time states
- Treat explicit transition language as decisive evidence: "yellow hair turned white," "years later," "recovered," "restored," "disguised as," "the house burned," and equivalent wording require a before/after decision. A source statement such as hair turns white from shock means the original hair color is the base and the white hair is a later state, even if the later look is mentioned first in the framing narrative.
- Most entities genuinely look the SAME throughout and should keep empty arrays, but never choose [] by default. Empty arrays are valid only after the inventory finds no explicit before/after change and no sustained visually distinct condition.
- Use states whenever an entity changes at a definite point or remains in a story-significant altered condition for one or more depictable beats. A state does not need to be permanent or span multiple scenes.
- Model it as a CATALOG of alternate looks (appearance_states) + an ordered TIMELINE of transitions (appearance_timeline).
- appearance_states[]: each {id, label, visual_anchor, baseline_affect, visually_significant, canonical_equivalent, continuity_guard, changed_facets, replaces_identity}. Always emit changed_facets and replaces_identity explicitly, including empty/false values. For changed hair color, changed_facets includes "hair"; an empty list would incorrectly preserve canonical hair from the reference image.
  - visual_anchor is SELF-CONTAINED physical identity: it fully describes the entity in that state (identity + the physical change) so it can REPLACE the base look with no contradiction. Never a bare delta; never mix two states' tags; never include emotion, expression, pose, or action.
  - A sustained mental or medical condition may justify a state even when the source gives few new physical traits. Keep its visual_anchor evidence-grounded and self-contained; record the sustained altered demeanor in baseline_affect instead of inventing sweat, wounds, clothing, or anatomy.
  - baseline_affect is optional and describes a sustained state-specific demeanor such as delirious, possessed, or shell-shocked. A momentary expression remains panel direction, not appearance identity.
  - visually_significant: true when the state deserves its own generated reference image (a body transformation, a collapsed building); false for a minor text-only difference (dusty travel clothes).
  - canonical_equivalent: true only for a return to the unchanged base physical look, such as recovery after a temporary fever. This lets the timeline explicitly leave the altered state while reusing canonical identity. A return-to-base state must set `"canonical_equivalent": true`; every physically changed look must set it to false.
- appearance_timeline[]: each {state_id, at, transition?}, ORDERED by story position.
  - at = {scene_id, beat}: the FIRST point where the new look holds. beat = null means "from that scene's start" (the usual case). Before the first activation, the base visual_anchor applies.
  - Never place two activations for one entity at the same scene/beat. Illness and recovery within a scene require distinct numbered beats or separate source-ordered scenes; two null beats would erase the illness interval.
  - A framing mention of a later appearance is not an activation in an earlier lived-time diary sequence. Explicitly return to a canonical-equivalent state at the start of an earlier flashback, then activate the changed state where its cause occurs in that sequence. Do not carry a survivor's white hair backward into the pre-trauma voyage.
  - Place `at` at the scene where the NEW look first clearly holds. If a destructive act must be SHOWN with the old look (the hand being cut), set `at` to the following scene.
  - transition (encouraged): {label, depicted_at, cause}. label = short name ("hands severed"); depicted_at = where it is shown; cause = why (kept for narration, does not change rendering).
- THINK IN TRANSITIONS: list every moment an entity enters or leaves an altered look, in order; each becomes one appearance_state + one timeline activation. For a temporary condition, create both the altered state and a later recovered/restored state. Never activate a temporary state without a subsequent exit when the source explicitly says it ends.
- CROSS-FIELD INVARIANT: whenever the source contains an explicit before/after change, the earlier condition must be the base (or the active earlier state), the later condition must have an appearance_state, and the timeline must activate it at the first depicted point where it holds. No changed feature may leak backward into the base anchor.
- WORKED EXAMPLE — temporary illness and recovery:
  A dark-haired young sculptor becomes feverish, alternates between unconsciousness and delirium, then recovers.
  appearance_states:
    {"id":"sculptor_delirious", "label":"Feverish delirium", "visual_anchor":"the complete evidence-grounded base physical tags", "baseline_affect":"alternating between unconsciousness and delirium", "visually_significant":true, "canonical_equivalent":false, "continuity_guard":"Keep him visibly incapacitated and disconnected from ordinary surroundings; never stage him as alert and conversational."}
    {"id":"sculptor_recovered", "label":"Recovered", "visual_anchor":"the complete base physical tags", "baseline_affect":"alert and returned to ordinary awareness", "visually_significant":false, "canonical_equivalent":true, "continuity_guard":null}
  appearance_timeline:
    {"state_id":"sculptor_delirious", "at":{"scene_id":"scene_003","beat":null}, "transition":{"label":"stricken by fever","cause":"the source's supernatural crisis"}}
    {"state_id":"sculptor_recovered", "at":{"scene_id":"scene_004","beat":null}, "transition":{"label":"malady suddenly ceases","cause":"the crisis ends"}}
- WORKED EXAMPLE — retrospective trauma:
  The framing narrative says a sailor returned with yellow hair turned white, then an embedded diary depicts the earlier voyage. Base visual_anchor uses yellow hair. Add a self-contained white-haired survivor state and activate it only after the traumatic event at the precise beat where the changed look first holds. The framing mention is evidence, not permission to render the earlier voyage with white hair.
- WORKED EXAMPLE — The Girl Without Hands, char_001 base anchor = a real-handed peasant girl:
  appearance_states: stumps, silver, restored (all visually_significant: true)
  appearance_timeline:
    {"state_id":"stumps",   "at":{"scene_id":"scene_004","beat":null}, "transition":{"label":"hands severed",      "cause":"her father's axe, at the Devil's command"}}
    {"state_id":"silver",   "at":{"scene_id":"scene_009","beat":null}, "transition":{"label":"silver hands forged", "cause":"the King has them made"}}
    {"state_id":"restored", "at":{"scene_id":"scene_016","beat":null}, "transition":{"label":"hands restored",      "cause":"divine grace"}}
  Result: scenes 1-3 show real hands, 4-8 stumps, 9-15 silver, 16 restored — and silver NEVER appears before scene_009.

SCENES
- List scenes in strict source-text order, never reconstructed chronology or
  dramatic chronology. Every source_start_quote must occur monotonically later
  in the source than the previous scene's quote. A flashback or embedded
  document stays where the framing narrative presents it.
- A new scene starts when ANY of the following changes: location, location appearance state, POV character, OR a significant time jump (>30 min, "later that day", flashback to earlier).
- source_start_quote: copy the complete opening sentence of the scene VERBATIM from the text — exact words, punctuation, capitalisation. This is used both to locate the scene in the source file and as its displayed excerpt. Do NOT invent, paraphrase, or stop mid-sentence.
- A character's or location's CHANGED appearance is NOT recorded on the scene. It lives on the entity's appearance_states + appearance_timeline (see APPEARANCE TIMELINE), which resolve per panel automatically.
- Target 5-15 scenes for a short story, 30-60 for a full novel. Be exhaustive — cover the ENTIRE novel from first page to last.
- Each scene should yield 3-8 comic panels. If a scene is too long OR spans an in/out transition OR jumps locations, SPLIT it.
- scene_type: one of action / dialogue / emotional / transition
- narrative_beat: one of inciting_incident / rising_action / midpoint / climax / falling_action / resolution

PROPS (recurring, multi-scene)
- Include any physical object that: (a) appears in 3+ scenes, OR (b) is central to the plot (a magic item, a weapon, a letter, a key)
- visual_anchor must describe what the object LOOKS LIKE in detail — size, shape, material, color, condition
- If the object is commonly misrepresented by image AI (e.g., a severed paw mistaken for a prosthetic), add an explicit "NOT" clause
- Leave props as an empty array [] if there are no recurring significant objects
- A prop is a DETACHABLE object that can be picked up, carried, or set down. A permanent change to a CHARACTER's body (a lost hand, a prosthetic, silver hands) is NOT a prop — model it as that character's appearance_state. A visible change to a PLACE is a location appearance_state. (Modeling silver hands as a prop is exactly what made them bleed onto every panel that merely mentioned hands.)

TRANSIENT OBJECTS (scene-scoped, one-time)
- A transient object is a story-critical visual element that appears in ONE scene only and is too narrow in scope to qualify as a Prop. Examples: a severed head visible for one beat, a floating alien text overlay during one announcement, a glowing portal that closes by scene's end.
- Include them under the scene's transient_objects list — not in the top-level props list.
- visual_anchor: 1-2 sentences describing the object's appearance. If it has a peculiar form the image model might misread, add a "NOT" clause.
- appears_in_beats: an optional list of 1-indexed beat numbers within the scene where the object is visible. Leave empty if it's visible throughout the scene.
- Do NOT promote a transient object to a Prop just because it's vivid — Props require multi-scene recurrence.

COMPLETENESS — CRITICAL
- You MUST extract EVERY named character who physically participates in 2+ scenes, not background-only mentions.
- You MUST also extract every named on-screen speaking character who carries
  story-critical testimony or explanation, even if they appear in only one
  scene.
- You MUST cover the full novel. Do not stop at chapter 10 if the novel has 20 chapters. Every chapter must be represented by at least one scene.
- You MUST finish the VISIBLE-CHANGE INVENTORY before emitting JSON. For every explicit before/after change, verify that the base anchor contains only the earlier look, each altered interval has a state, every state has a timeline activation, and every explicitly ended temporary state has a recovered/restored activation.

NARRATIVE ARCS
- Always output exactly 3 acts.
- act field is an integer: 1, 2, or 3.
- scenes field is an array of scene id strings listing every scene in that act.
- Every scene_id in the scenes array must appear in the top-level scenes array.

Output only the JSON object. No markdown fences. No explanation.

EXPLICIT VISUAL STATE CONTRACT
For every character, location, and recurring prop state, return changed_facets (only the physical facets actually changed) and replaces_identity (true only for an explicit total identity replacement). Do not infer replacement from creature names. Supported facets: age, hair, face, build, anatomy, clothing, damage, weather, light, water_level, architecture, landmarks, materials, atmosphere, shape, contents, color. Props use the same appearance_states and appearance_timeline as characters and locations: record opening, breakage, emptying, and other story-relevant visible changes. Keep self-contained visual_anchor text separate from these structured fields.

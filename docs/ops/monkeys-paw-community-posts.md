# The Monkey's Paw community sharing kit

**Status: Prepared October 15, 2026. Drafts; nothing posted.**

The showcase catalogue now includes *The Monkey's Paw* by W. W. Jacobs: 20
story pages, the seventh homepage book. The story (1902) is in the public
domain, so no licence notice is required; the drafts still credit Jacobs.

Public sharing URL after deployment:
[Read The Monkey's Paw](https://scenewright.ink/read/b32d070d85f942278577140e7f55778b).
This production URL was not verified live. Deploy the catalogue and check the
reader before posting. The localhost project URL is private and does not work
for community readers.

## Where to share

Reuse the communities vetted for
[Astronomical](astronomical-community-posts.md#where-to-share): r/SideProject,
the r/aiArt self-promotion thread, and r/AIcomics (approved contributors only).
Recheck each community's current rules and flair before posting. Do not post the
same link to the r/aiArt thread within seven days of the Astronomical comment.
Horror and comics communities often ban AI-generated work; read their rules
before considering them.

## Draft 1 — r/SideProject

**Title:** I built a prose-to-comic studio. Here's a complete 20-page adaptation of The Monkey's Paw.

I'm building Scenewright, a studio that turns prose into finished comic pages.
You pick the look, approve the cast, review the pages, and share the book.

This is W. W. Jacobs' *The Monkey's Paw*: an old soldier leaves a family a
shrivelled paw that grants three wishes, and begs them not to use it. The art
and lettering are AI-generated; the text follows Jacobs' original dialogue.

The full comic is readable without an account:
[Read The Monkey's Paw](https://scenewright.ink/read/b32d070d85f942278577140e7f55778b).

I'd love feedback on two things: does the dread build from page to page, and
would you use something like this to adapt your own writing?

## Draft 3 — r/AIcomics

**Title:** The Monkey's Paw — a 20-page AI-assisted adaptation of the 1902 horror classic

A retired sergeant-major brings home a mummified paw from India. It grants
three wishes, he says, and the last man who used it wished for death. The
White family keeps it anyway.

I made this with Scenewright, the prose-to-comic studio I'm building. The art
and lettering are AI-generated; the story is W. W. Jacobs' public-domain
original.

[Read all 20 pages here, no account needed](https://scenewright.ink/read/b32d070d85f942278577140e7f55778b).

How does the pacing land, especially the last few pages? I'm trying to learn
whether silent panels and sound effects carry the tension the way prose does.

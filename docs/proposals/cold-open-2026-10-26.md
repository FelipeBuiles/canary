# Cold Open: the author's story as a comic page in about a minute

**Status: Implemented, October 27, 2026; not yet run against paid providers.**
Every path is covered by provider-free tests and the full backend and frontend
gates. The reveal was checked in the browser with stubbed data. No house look
has been generated, so their visual quality is unverified (see
[Before shipping](#before-shipping)).

## Goal

Today, an author first sees their own story drawn as a comic after Extract, a
Brief approval and the whole Style stage. The Cold Open shows **one page of
their opening in three looks while Extract is still reading the rest of the
book**, before any decision, with real lines from the passage in its balloons.
Canary pays for it.

Measure: the median time from upload to the first rendered page of the
author's own story. Target: under two minutes, with no decisions. The
`cold_open_printed` product event records the time from snapshot to pages
(`latency_ms`). Join it with `project_created` for the whole path.

## The flow

```
upload ─▶ Extract section 1 (~42 s) ─▶ source/opening.json + "opening" snapshot
                                        │
          server orchestration ─────────┴─▶ Cold Open job (sponsored, 0 credits)
                                              ├─ rank looks  ┐ parallel, ~4 s
                                              ├─ proof brief + story copy ┘
                                              └─ 3 proofs, parallel (~22–25 s)
          Extract sections 2..n continue ─▶ Brief ─▶ [approve] ─▶ Style
                                                               └─ adopts identical proofs
```

The replay projects **about 68 s at p50 and 133 s at p90** after upload. A short
book (one Extract call) publishes its opening when Extract finishes, and the
Cold Open then runs alongside Brief.

### What the author sees

Each placement follows what the author is doing at that moment.

- **Story room, while Extract and Brief run:** the wait is the moment for the
  reveal. A dark press bed sits above the stage progress: "Your first page is
  on the press". It shows the chapter and scene, then three sheets developing
  through the streamed image previews and landing one by one. An "On the
  house" stamp; no credits are shown. "This one" marks a favorite and slams a
  "Your pick" stamp on the sheet. That is a preference, never a selection.
- **Beside the Brief, once it needs review:** the Brief is the task, so the
  press becomes a quiet "First pages" card in the right rail, after "Before
  you approve". It shows the printed thumbnails (or a printed count while the
  press runs), the scene, the pick if there is one, and, once the Brief is
  approved, "Make the whole book look like this". Dropped pages are left out.
- **In Look's empty room:** "Your first pages" sit between the intro and the
  two ways to start ("Art-direct for me", "Bring your own"), where picking a
  favorite actually matters. Look curates its own six directions; proofs that
  match its scene join the picker at no cost, labeled "First pages", with the
  favorite first and marked "Your pick" in gold.

### Ambient work

The Cold Open is Canary's own work beside the pipeline (`AMBIENT_JOB_KINDS`).
Admission, `project_busy`, the status API's `active_jobs` and stage status all
ignore it. Approving the Brief, running a stage or starting Look is never held
back by it, and the floating progress indicator never reports it as Look.
Deleting the project still waits for it, because it writes into the project.
It runs in the account's image lane, so Look work started while pages print
queues behind it and finds them finished. The cost: account image work in
another project delays it.

### Who pays

Canary absorbs the first Cold Open per account (`CANARY_COLD_OPEN_PER_ACCOUNT`;
unset, it is 1 in production and unlimited in every other environment; 0
turns it off). The grant is recorded in the account folder
(`.cold_open_grants.json`), so deleting the project does not return it. A
project gets at most one Cold Open, and none once Style has proofs of its own.

Every call in the job runs with `AIRequestContext.sponsor = "cold_open"`. The AI
client then skips the per-call credit authorization and never debits. Usage
events still record provider cost, with `sponsor` in their metadata. The job
command reserves 0 credits, and `JobCommand` rejects a sponsor on any other
operation. The record keeps the absorbed provider cost and counts calls with
unknown cost; neither is shown to the author.

## Seams and boundaries

1. **Extract.** `publish_opening` writes `OpeningSnapshot` (the Story Bible after
   section 1 plus that section's text) and publishes an `opening` stage
   snapshot. Visual anchors are already final: a later section cannot change
   one (`RegistryAnchorConflict`). The snapshot is not an authority, and no
   stage reads it.
2. **Orchestration.** `_run_stage_with_credits` observes Extract's events.
   `start_cold_open` admits the job and never raises into the Extract run.
   Stages still do not call one another.
3. **Style proof machinery, reused.** The job calls `ensure_style_sample_brief`
   and `generate_sample_image` exactly as Style does. One Style change was
   needed: proof copy accepts the scene `passage` explicitly. Mid-Extract the
   source document does not exist yet, and its cache key ignores the passage,
   so without it Style would later have reused lines invented from a summary.
   `opening_passage` cuts the scene's text from the snapshot. The record keeps
   its own copy of the lines, so a later Style cache refresh never relabels
   the pages.
4. **Look choice.** One small prompt-model call (`cold_open.rank_looks`) ranks
   the nine house looks by fit with the opening, in parallel with the brief.
   `choose_looks` then enforces distinct medium families and color strategies
   and always includes one comic. The word-overlap match is the fallback when
   the call fails.
5. **Adoption.** `adoptable_looks` builds the request Style would make now from
   the published Story Bible and Style's own caches. It adopts a Cold Open
   proof only when that request's fingerprint matches the proof's. Either
   curation path (the stage run or the Look room's suggest job) then copies
   the proof and its metadata into the picker, under `production_lock`, at no
   cost. A mismatch, such as a different proof scene or anchor or a changed
   setting, stays a first impression and is never adopted. With nothing to
   adopt, Style's curation call is unchanged.

### Guardrails kept

- No retries. A failed proof is shown as failed, its siblings survive, and an
  interrupted job is reported, never rerun. The approved Sunburst
  OpenAI → FAL attempt inside image generation still applies.
- No style is selected for the author, so `style_selected` gating is unchanged.
- Estimates, holds, absorbed cost and charges stay distinct.

## Estimator fix

`estimate_text_cost` added the route's flat thinking allowance (4,096 tokens on
Argon, Flash and Claude) to every call. Over the 45 days to October 27, Argon
Style briefs and proof copy billed at most 160 output tokens in total, while
Flash occasionally spent about 1,500 thinking tokens on the same calls. The
allowance is now `min(route ceiling, 1,024 + answer + input / 4)`. It covers
those calls and lowers every small-call estimate; no estimate rises.
For the Cold Open, the replay falls from **~10–19 credits to 10–14 (median 13)**.
Image reservations now dominate.

## Replay results

`novel_to_comic/.venv/bin/python scripts/prototype_cold_open.py projects/<user>/*/`
ran on 16 local series. Seven were skipped because their Story Bibles predate
part sequences. In 8 of 9, the proof request matched Style's. In dcc, Style's
opening window reaches past section 1 and picks a different scene; the
fingerprint gate now handles that case without adopting.

## Open decisions

1. **Scene choice.** Style's own rule keeps adoption simple. Scoring the most
   striking scene could produce a stronger page, but it would lose adoption.
   In dcc, the chosen scene's summary began with "front matter".
2. **Later accounts and projects.** Only the first project per account gets a
   Cold Open. A paid `~N credits` Cold Open for later projects is a natural
   next step.

## Observed in the first local run (October 27)

On "Nightmare at 20,000 feet", the job took 6.5 minutes because the local image
route is the Codex subscription adapter, which queues requests. One of three
pages was declined (`provider_blocked`): the proof scene shows the lead
contemplating suicide with a pistol. Style's proof-scene rule does not avoid
sensitive scenes, so a first impression can land on one.

Pre-launch review · 18 July 2026

Prove a paid author outcome before you “launch.”

Scenewright has a credible product story and a functioning production system. What it does not yet have is a safe, observable, repeatable path from interested author to successful paying customer.

Recommendation Do not open public signup yet. Run a 10-customer Founding Author Lab after the release and operations gates pass.

01 · Context

The strategy is directionally right; the launch plan is not operational enough.

This review combines the existing branding proposal, the current repository and live environment, recent CI evidence, and current public competitor and author-market evidence. It does not include CRM data, customer interviews, support logs, or conversion data—because those systems and cohorts do not exist yet.

LiveFly machine is running; platform check passes and the readiness endpoint reports Supabase and LiteLLM as healthy.
Not releasableThe working tree is heavily modified and the latest visible CI runs are failing.
Not hardenedThe live homepage response lacks the browser-security headers called for by the launch gate.
Not observableNo acquisition attribution, product funnel, lifecycle email, referral, or structured feedback system was found.
Immediate contradiction to fix: the product says a sample story is the “zero-friction first success,” but no automatic signup credit grant was found. A new user can choose a sample and then encounter a credit wall before experiencing the value.

02 · Seller baseline

Keep the positioning. Change the entry outcome.

Keep

Durable product truths

  • Whole-manuscript ingestion and a source-faithful adaptation workflow.
  • Four understandable author decisions: story, look, panels, book.
  • Reference-guided cast and location continuity with focused panel correction.
  • Lettering and page finishing inside the same system.
  • Explicit cost estimates, non-expiring credits, and no subscription.
  • Precise rights language: manuscript ownership remains with the author; generated-output copyrightability is case-specific.
Change

The first job to be done

Do not ask an unproven customer to commit to a full graphic novel. Sell a finished visual reader magnet or first-chapter issue: a 6–12 page artifact they can share with readers, use in a newsletter, or include in a launch campaign.

This preserves the whole-book vision while making the first purchase faster, cheaper, measurable, and naturally distributable.

Sharper promise: “Turn one chapter or bonus scene into a graphic issue you direct—then continue the book when the cast, look, and workflow have earned your trust.”

04 · Pre-launch backlog

Seven gates before inviting real authors.

P0.1

Release a known-good revision

Fix CI’s missing Playwright browser setup, require all jobs to pass for the exact release SHA, remove or isolate unrelated working-tree changes, and ensure the deployed image maps to that SHA.

Exit: green CI + reproducible deployment + recorded release SHA.
P0.2

Finish the operational acceptance test

Complete two-user authorization, email confirmation/reset, Stripe test purchase and duplicate webhook, end-to-end manuscript run, reservation recovery, Sentry alert, snapshot restore, mobile/error flows, and language scope. Add CSP, HSTS, nosniff, referrer, and frame policies; bound decompressed imports.

Exit: signed checklist with evidence, owners, and rollback notes.
P0.3

Make first value reachable

Choose one: grant tightly capped trial credits to accepted design partners, or sell the $12 Starter pilot before generation. The public CTA must never imply a usable sample if the user will immediately hit a zero balance.

Exit: a new invited user can reach a first generated panel without surprise.
P0.4

Add first-party learning and attribution

Capture consent-aware UTMs and a small event vocabulary: landing, application/signup, confirmation, project created, extract approved, style selected, cast approved, first panel, first export, purchase, support issue, referral, and deletion. Never send manuscript text, prompts, images, auth data, or entity names to analytics.

Exit: one funnel report by source and cohort; cost and gross margin joined to project outcomes.
P0.5

Prove the product with permissioned work

Run three public-domain benchmarks and 5–10 author-owned pilots. Measure continuity, panel flag rate, successful export, actual credits, retries, elapsed time, and operator intervention. Secure explicit permission before using any manuscript, image, name, result, or quote.

Exit: one 30–50-panel proof reel and two honest case studies.
P0.6

Close trust and support operations

Monitor support and legal inboxes; add an in-product “report a problem” route with project/stage metadata; document refund, abuse/DMCA, deletion/export, failed-generation, and incident handling. Publish an AI-use and KDP-disclosure guide without legal promises.

Exit: support response target, runbooks, and working contacts.
P0.7

Resolve the name risk

Have a practitioner assess SceneWrite/Scenewright across federal, state, common-law, domain, and relevant international use. Until then, avoid paid brand campaigns, major assets, or wide press.

Exit: written go/rename recommendation before public promotion.

05 · Initial sales motion

Founding Author Lab: ten paying customers, two at a time.

Who

Narrow ICP

Self-published fantasy, science-fiction, horror, or romance authors with a finished book, clear rights, at least two published titles or an active serial, an existing reader channel, and an explicit willingness to use generative tools.

Offer

$12 visual pilot

Use the existing Starter pack as the price anchor. Promise a 6–12 page visual reader magnet or first-chapter issue, one style direction, one cast, panel repair, and PDF/CBZ export. Credits remain theirs.

Exchange

Learning contract

In return for founding access: a short intake, structured feedback after export, and permission to use anonymized operational metrics. Quote or artwork permission is separate and optional.

Recruit in this order

  1. Warm and adjacent network: authors, editors, cover designers, formatters, writing coaches, and author assistants who can make trusted introductions.
  2. High-intent hand research: authors publicly discussing visual adaptations, AI-assisted publishing, reader magnets, bonus chapters, or comic editions. Use public business contact details; review every message manually.
  3. Partner referrals: give cover designers and author marketers a co-branded visual-pilot link and fixed bonus credits after a referred customer pays and exports.
  4. Permissioned project content: turn each successful pilot into a before/after strip, continuity sheet, cost/time summary, and author quote; the author approves every asset before publication.
  5. High-intent search: publish real example pages for “turn a novel into a graphic novel,” “book to comic,” and “visual reader magnet for authors.” Avoid generic “AI comic generator” traffic where incumbents are stronger.
Do not automate community posting or mass DMs. Automate research, scoring, drafts, reminders, and reporting. Keep targeting, claims, and sending under human review; trust is the scarce resource in this segment.

06 · Automated acquisition system

Automate the plumbing; keep the relationship human.

1 · AttractProof pagesPublic examples, cost calculator, adaptation guide, visual-reader-magnet guide.
2 · CaptureQualified applicationGenre, rights, word count, audience, goal, AI posture, source/UTM.
3 · ScoreFit routingAuto-score 0–100; human approves. Reject or nurture low-fit leads.
4 · ActivateTriggered checklistWelcome, rights check, sample choice, credit state, next gate, stalled-user reminders.
5 · ConvertOutcome-based offer$12 Starter pilot; $30 Standard only after a successful first export.
6 · CompoundProof + referralAsk for approved assets, referral link, and next-book continuation.

Minimal data model

  • leads: source, consent, fit fields, score, status, owner.
  • campaign touches: channel, template, human approval, sent/replied dates.
  • product events: user/project ID, event, stage, timestamp, cohort; no creative content.
  • customer outcomes: export, pages, credits, retries, flags, support minutes, permission status.
  • referrals: referrer, referred account, paid/exported qualification, reward.

Trigger set

  • Signup but no project in 24 hours → one orientation message.
  • Project created but no extract approval in 48 hours → contextual help.
  • Cast approved → explain the automated panel chain and expected timing/cost.
  • First panel generated → ask one confidence question, not a long survey.
  • Export completed → case-study permission, referral, and next-step offer.
  • Failure or repeated regeneration → support alert; suppress promotional email.

07 · Six-week plan and go/no-go

Launch only when the system produces customers, proof, and learning.

Week 1
Release safety. Fix CI, identify the deployed SHA, complete security headers, Sentry, support contacts, and the acceptance-test owners.
Evidence: green release, live alert, health and rollback records.
Week 2
Observability and first value. Add UTMs/events, design-partner application, fit scoring, a visible trial/pilot credit state, and triggered transactional messaging.
Evidence: test account appears end-to-end in the funnel report.
Week 3
Proof production. Complete public-domain benchmarks and recruit the first four qualified authors. Run no more than two concurrently.
Evidence: first 30–50 panel reel and measured cost/retry data.
Weeks 4–5
Founding Author Lab. Enroll ten authors into the $12 pilot, in waves. Conduct short interviews only at start and after export; collect in-product micro-feedback between.
Evidence: paid receipts, exports, friction tags, support time.
Week 6
Decide. Publish only permissioned proof, fix the top two shared blockers, and decide whether to expand to 25 invitees, repeat the lab, or pause.
Evidence: cohort scorecard and explicit go/no-go memo.

08 · What not to overclaim

Trust language is part of the product.

Do not say

  • Perfect or guaranteed character consistency
  • Copyright-safe or copyright-protected
  • KDP-ready without disclosure
  • A full novel in minutes
  • A fixed cost per book
  • Publish-ready without qualification

Say instead

  • Reference-guided continuity with focused repair
  • Human direction and arrangement are documented
  • KDP treats generated interior images as AI-generated content
  • Costs are estimated before each paid step
  • Export PDF and CBZ for further review and production

Still unknown

  • Best-converting genre and buyer profile
  • True retry and panel-repair rate
  • Support minutes per completed project
  • Visitor-to-paid conversion
  • Referral coefficient
  • Whether the Scenewright name is defensible

# The Director: when a note should go further than one page

**Status: Implemented, October 27, 2026; not yet run against paid providers.**
All four slices are built and covered by provider-free tests. No paid change
check, fact check, beat revision or review has run, and the carry-through card
has not been seen in the browser (see [What was built](#what-was-built)).

## Verdict

The Director as first pitched does **not** belong: one conversation, across
all four stations, that can change anything in the book. A narrower form does
belong, and it is the part that made the idea attractive: **an author's note
goes as far as it needs to, and no further.**

The author keeps writing notes where they already do, in the page change box.
Most notes are about that page, and nothing about them changes. When a note
states a fact that holds beyond the page ("she took her apron off a few pages
back"), or asks for something a redraw cannot do ("this line is hers, not
his"), Canary says so. It names the layer that owns the fact, lists every page
the fact touches and shows the cost, and then makes the change with one
confirmation.

There is no new panel, chat or station. The Director is never on screen
unless a note needs it.

## Why not a conversation

- **It competes with the rooms.** Product principle 1 is "the tool disappears
  into the task". A chat beside four dense work surfaces is a second way to do
  everything, and it would be the vaguer one.
- **Its blast radius is the whole project.** A chat that can do anything must
  be as safe as every room combined. Each room's checks (Brief approval,
  revision-checked Story Bible edits, the "never remove what a part cites"
  rule, fingerprinted Draw inputs) would have to be rebuilt behind one text
  box.
- **The book-wide seam already exists, and nothing uses it.**
  `story-changes/preview` and `apply` ([editorial.py](../../novel_to_comic/src/novel_to_comic/editorial.py))
  take one instruction and re-run Adapt and Plan for the whole part. Every
  page may go out of date and the page count can change. No UI calls it,
  which is the right call: that is not a button an author should press over a
  line of dialogue. A conversational Director would mostly end up routing
  into it.
- **Authors don't converse; they point.** Every note in the evidence below
  starts from a specific page they were looking at.

## Evidence

Usage is small: `page_outcome` events over 60 days come from two authors. The
counts can't settle this. The notes themselves can. Ten local projects hold
about 50 notes written by hand (`draw/pages/page_*/issues.json`, type
`manual_change`). They fall into three groups:

| Group | Share | Examples | What happens today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Page-local art | ~60% | "remove the extra hand", "three fingers up", "the boy's pose is strange", "dialogue tail should point to the lady" | Page correction. Works. |
| One fact, many pages | ~20% | "she should not be wearing the apron, she took it off a few panels back" (filed separately on pages 17, 18 and 19), "move the staircase to the right side of the door like on page 20", "keep all three panels in Testing Chamber 142", "remove his hat while he's in bed" | The author finds and fixes each page alone, by hand. |
| Story or script | ~20% | "it is not clear the sergeant threw the paw at the fireplace, rewrite it and redraw" (filed on two versions of the book), "there is no panel conveying this news", "this dialogue should be said by her in the first panel", "Fix the typo, it should say *He paused to strike another.*" | The change checker says "BELONGS TO THE SCRIPT: change this line in Story, then re-plan the page". No screen can change a line of the script, so this is a dead end. Authors redraw anyway, which cannot work. |

The apron shows the size of the gap. In *The Monkey's Paw*, Mrs. White appears
on **11 pages from page 17 to the end**. The author found the apron on three
of them and filed three notes. The other eight were never checked. One fact
should have been one note.

## What it does

### The note box reads scope

The page change box already sends notes to the change checker
([correction_interpreter.py](../../novel_to_comic/src/novel_to_comic/correction_interpreter.py)),
which routes each one to `draw`, `plan` or `script`. Two changes:

1. **The checker states facts, it doesn't find pages.** Beside the route, a
   reading can name a typed claim: an entity, a look or place detail, and
   where in the story it starts or ends. It never lists page numbers.
2. **Code finds the pages.** A provider-free resolver reads the page plan.
   Panels already bind each visible entity to its look
   (`PageContent.appearance_states`), and beats already record appearance
   changes (`AdaptedBeat` / `AppearanceChange`). From those, the resolver
   lists every page and panel the claim touches.

**The model names the fact; code finds the pages.** The page list is the
part the author has to trust, and a model guessing page numbers is exactly
how a project gets quietly damaged.

### "This goes beyond page 17"

When a reading reaches past its page, the change checker shows one card in
place of the usual suggestion:

> **This goes beyond page 17.** Mrs. White has no apron from page 17, where
> she takes it off, to the end of Part 1. She appears on 11 of those pages.
>
> **Carry it through** · checks 11 pages, redraws those that need it

Confirming opens the usual confirm drawer with the full plan, grouped by
owner, each line in the room's own words:

```
Story Bible   + Mrs. White: new look "Without apron"
Script        beat 41: Mrs. White takes off her apron
Page plan     14 panels on 11 pages show her in the new look
Pages         check 11 pages for the apron  ~N credits
              redraw the pages that still show it  priced after the check
```

The author can drop pages from the list. A dropped page keeps its art and is
marked out of date, as any page already is after a plan change.

### Check first, then redraw

Redrawing 11 pages because three were wrong is how a "helpful" feature spends
someone's credits. A fact that holds over a range is checked against the
pages' current art first, using the existing visual review, which already
works against bindings. Only pages that break the fact are offered for redraw,
in a second, priced step. For the three pages the author flagged, the check is
skipped and they go straight to the redraw list.

### The three kinds

| Kind | From the note | Owner chain | Model calls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Continuity**: a look or place fact over a range | "apron off from here", "hat off in bed", "staircase on the right like page 20" | Story Bible (new look, or an edited place detail) → Adapt (an appearance change at the beat) → Plan (rebind those panels) → check → redraw | None for the edits. An optional Cast image of the new look. Checks and redraws are priced as today. |
| **Line**: wording or speaker | typo, "her line, not his", "move this line to panel 1" | Adapt (edit that `LetteringItem`) → Plan (move it between panels on that page) → Draw for integrated editions, lettering only for editable ones | None. These are typed edits. |
| **Beat**: missing or unclear story moment | "no panel conveys the news", "make the throw clear" | Adapt (repair only the shard that holds those source units, with the note) → Plan (re-plan the pages that cite the changed beats) → redraw | One Adapt shard repair, and one re-plan per page |

Page-local art notes stay exactly as they are.

## What it never does

- Never runs without the confirm drawer: every owner and page listed, `~N
  credits`, an explicit confirmation.
- Never re-adapts or re-plans the whole part. If a change can't be held to the
  pages it touches (a beat no longer fits its page), it stops and says which
  pages need a re-plan.
- Never edits an approved Brief or the selected Style.
- Never removes or merges what an adapted part cites (ADR 0023). It only adds
  looks and edits details.
- Never changes a page the author didn't see listed.
- Never applies part of a change. It writes the Story Bible, Adapt and Plan
  revisions as one change, or none of them. Redraws are separate jobs, and
  each page succeeds or fails on its own.
- Never retries a paid redraw automatically.

## Undo

Every applied note is one **change record** listing the revisions it wrote.
Undo restores the Story Bible revision (`story-bible/undo` exists), the
adaptation and the plan pages. Redrawn pages keep earlier renders selectable
(`renders/{revision}/select` exists), so undo never deletes art. The record
appears as one entry in page history: "Carried through: Mrs. White without
apron, 11 pages".

## Architecture

```
page change box ─▶ change checker (1 call, as today)
                      └─ reading: route + optional claim {entity, detail, starts_at, ends_at}
                             │
                    resolver (provider-free): claim × plan × adaptation
                             └─ ChangePlan {owners, pages, panels, check list, redraw list}
                                    │  preview: persisted, revision-stamped, no writes
                                    ▼
                    apply (server orchestration, one project lock)
                      ├─ Story Bible edit   (revision-checked, existing routes' logic)
                      ├─ Adapt amendment    (typed beat/lettering edit, or one shard repair)
                      ├─ Plan amendment     (panel rebinds, or per-page re-plan)
                      └─ queue: visual checks → priced redraw offer
```

- **New module `note_scope.py`**: claim types, the resolver and `ChangePlan`.
  It has no I/O and makes no model calls.
- **New `TargetArtifacts` reads and writes**: `ChangeRecord` (the preview and
  then the applied record). The stamp is the Story Bible revision, the
  adaptation revision and the plan revision. Apply refuses with 409 if any of
  them moved.
- **Adapt and Plan amendments are new revisions** of the existing artifacts,
  validated by the same `validate_*` code as a stage run, including the
  source ledger. Stages don't call one another; the server applies them in
  order.
- **Existing `story-changes` preview/apply**: delete it once Line and Beat
  amendments land. It has no caller, and its blast radius is the reason the
  rest of this design exists. (Forward-only: no shim.)

## Cost

- Reading a note: unchanged. The change checker already runs.
- Continuity and Line edits: no model calls.
- Optional Cast image of a new look: the existing `regen-state-ref` estimate.
- Checking pages: the existing visual review estimate, times the pages not
  flagged by the author.
- Beat repair: one Adapt shard call, plus one `replan` per affected page,
  priced by the existing estimators.
- Redraws: the existing redraw estimate, offered only after the check, for
  the pages that need it.

The confirm drawer sums these with the existing estimate functions. It adds
no pricing of its own.

## Build order

Each slice ships on its own and can be stopped after.

1. **Line amendments.** This closes the "BELONGS TO THE SCRIPT" dead end with
   the smallest, fully typed change: wording, speaker or panel of one line on
   one page. Done when the typo and "her line, not his" notes above can be
   applied from the page and the page redraws or relets with the right copy.
2. **Continuity amendments with check-then-redraw.** The apron case. Done
   when one note on page 17 adds the look, rebinds the panels, checks the
   other pages and offers redraws only where the apron still shows. This is
   where most of the value is.
3. **Beat amendments.** Done when "there is no panel conveying this news"
   repairs the shard and re-plans only the pages that cite it, or stops and
   names the pages that need a re-plan.
4. **Delete `story-changes` preview/apply.**

## How we'll know

Powerful, or not there. Track with `page_outcome`:

- **Repeat notes**: the same fact filed on several pages (the apron
  pattern). Should drop to near zero.
- **Script dead ends**: readings routed to `script` with no follow-up
  action. Should drop to zero after slice 1.
- **Carry-through accept rate**: how many "This goes beyond page N" cards are
  confirmed. If authors mostly dismiss them, the claim detection is wrong or
  the card is noise. Fix it or remove it; never leave it in place.
- **Undo rate on applied changes**: a high rate means the resolver's page
  lists are wrong.

## What was built

- **Change check** (`correction_interpreter.py`): each reading may name what it
  reaches (`reach`, a line id with new wording or speaker, a panel for a story
  moment, or an entity with a fact and a new or existing look).
  `claim_for` turns that into a typed claim; the route returns it as `claim`.
- **Scope and amendments** (`note_scope.py`): provider-free page lists and
  amended artifacts for each claim.
- **Model calls** (`note_calls.py`): beat revision and the per-page fact check.
- **Orchestration** (`server/note_changes.py`, `server/routers/notes.py`):
  preview, checks, apply, undo; `NoteChange` records in `notes/`.
- **UI** (`web/src/components/pages/CarryThrough.tsx`): replaces the "change
  this line in Story" dead end, and appears under any reading whose fact
  reaches beyond the page.
- **Removed**: `story-changes` preview/apply, proposal and directive storage,
  and Adapt/Plan directive consumption.

### Where it differs from the design above

- **Looks don't amend the script.** Plan validation enforces appearance
  chronology: once the adaptation says a look changes, every later panel must
  show it, which would put the whole run out of date. A look is instead added
  to the Story Bible with its timeline starting at the start page's scene
  (which a later Adapt reads), and only the chosen pages' panels are bound to
  it. Pages whose art already honours the fact are left alone.
- **Page pins are now beat-scoped.** `BookAdaptation.amendments` records each
  scoped script change; `ComicPage.cites_adaptation` accepts a page pinned to
  an earlier revision while none of its cited beats changed. This is a change
  to what a pin promises, not a compatibility exception: a full Adapt run
  produces an adaptation with no amendments, and every page must then match it
  exactly, as before. Without it, any one-line change would put every page out
  of date.
- **Adding a look reopens Cast**, as adding one in the Story Bible already
  does. A recurring character's new look needs its Cast image before its pages
  can be redrawn; the card says so, but doesn't draw it.
- **Editable editions redraw for a wording change.** A line change makes the
  page's art out of date even when the art carries no lettering, because
  render freshness compares the whole ComicPage. Fixing that needs a
  freshness rule based on what Draw actually sends.
- **The internal `story_instruction` parameter stays** in Adapt and Plan at its
  empty default. It is part of Adapt's work-input hashes and the review cache
  keys, so removing it would invalidate checkpoints of in-flight work.

## Open decisions

- **Cast image for a new look**: draw it by default (more consistent pages,
  one extra image) or only on request.
- **Editable editions and line changes**: a canonical copy change re-seeds
  that page's editable lettering. Should it keep any hand-placed balloons the
  author moved, and only replace the text?
- **Ranges across parts**: a place detail lives in the series Story Bible, so
  editing it affects every part. Should the drawer offer "this part only" by
  adding a look, or "every part" by editing the detail?

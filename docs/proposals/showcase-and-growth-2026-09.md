## The idea in one paragraph

Scenewright's output is itself marketing material. A writer who turns a chapter into a comic gets something they want to post, and every post shows other writers what the product does. So the strategy has two engines. The first is a **showcase engine** of rights-cleared books, flipbooks and short videos that proves "your novel in any style" faster than any explanation. The second is a **creator loop** that makes each author's finished comic easy to share, with a link back. Paid media comes last and only amplifies what already works organically.

Avoid spending effort converting AI-hostile communities (many traditional-publishing and illustrator spaces). Be transparent everywhere; don't pick fights.

## Positioning

**Tagline:** Your novel. Any style.

**What it is:** a comic studio for fiction writers. Bring your manuscript, describe or show the look you want, approve the cast, and get finished, lettered pages you can revise in plain words, share in a public reader, or export as PDF and CBZ.

**Proof pillars** (each has a showcase asset behind it):

1. **Any look you can describe.** Twenty-six real proofs of one Poe scene, from woodblock to 16-bit pixel art.
2. **A whole book, not one image.** Finished editions of 44–56 pages in one chosen look.
3. **The same faces on every page.** Approved character references carry through the edition.
4. **Real lettering.** Integrated lettering painted into the art, or an editable layer with voices, tails and effects that can be translated.
5. **You direct.** Story brief, look, cast and page fixes are the author's decisions; corrections are plain-language requests.

**Claims we never make:** costs, credits or free generations in marketing media; sales-lift promises; "copyright-safe"; comparisons to named competitors without a like-for-like test; any artist's name as a style label.

### Formats that sell the product

- **The style flipbook:** one scene, many looks, accelerating. This is the strongest hook we have and it works at 6, 15 and 65 seconds.
- **Carousels:** "This Poe scene in 10 styles" as an Instagram/LinkedIn carousel, one proof per slide, and a TikTok photo post.
- **Comment a style:** post the scene, ask followers to comment a style, reply with a video of that proof. Cheap, participatory and endlessly repeatable.
- **Serialized classics:** one page a day of the Masque edition through October, ending on Halloween.
- **Before and after:** a plain-words revision (request typed, page redrawn) in under 8 seconds.
- **Lettering close-ups:** the editable layer toggling on and off, voices changing. Quietly satisfying, good for Shorts.
- **Build in public:** the founder's account showing how the pipeline, lettering engine and continuity work. This is the Hacker News and indie-maker angle.

## The creator loop

Most growth should come from what authors make, not from what we say. These product changes turn every finished comic into distribution:

1. **Share cards.** Public-reader links need proper Open Graph images: the cover plus the page count and title.
2. **"Export as a Reel."** Render a 9:16 page-flip video of the author's own comic (and a style flipbook of their scene) with the Remotion pipeline built for the showcase. Authors post it; the last frame credits Scenewright.
3. **Embeddable reader.** A small iframe for author websites and newsletters.
4. **Attribution.** A `ref` parameter on reader links and a "Made with Scenewright" end card, so we can count reader views that turn into sign-ups.
5. **Affiliate program.** Authors earn a share of first purchases they refer. Writers already run affiliate links in newsletters.
6. **Author Spotlight.** Adapt the opening chapter of 10–20 AI-open indie authors (10k–100k followers) with written permission. They share it, we feature it, and we get real proof plus honest feedback.

## Measurement

- **North star:** authors who finish and share an edition each week.
- **Activation:** a new sign-up sees their first style proof in the first session.
- **Conversion:** first purchase within 7 days of first proof.
- **Loop health:** public-reader views per shared edition, and sign-ups per 1,000 reader views.
- **Channel:** cost per activated author and first-purchase payback, reported per channel and per creative.
- **Content:** watch-through past the 3-second hook, saves and shares; comments that request styles.

Minimum instrumentation before any spend: campaign parameters captured at sign-up; first proof, first purchase, first export and first share recorded server-side; reader-link `ref` attribution.

**Starting targets for October.** These are guesses to beat, not forecasts; replace them with real baselines after two weeks of posting.

| Signal | Target |
|---|---|
| Short videos watched past 3 s | 35% or more |
| Saves plus shares per 1,000 views | 10 or more |
| Style requests per comment-a-style post | 20 or more |
| Spotlight authors who accept | 3 of the first 10 invited |
| Sign-ups who see a first style proof in the first session | 60% or more |

**Kill or scale rule:** after four posts in a format, drop it if it sits below half the target on both watch-through and shares; make two more variants of anything above target.

## Risks and guardrails

- **AI-art backlash.** Label work "AI-assisted, author-directed"; never use artist names (see [finding #1](../../showcase/FINDINGS-2026-09-26.md)); don't promote in anti-AI spaces.
- **Rights.** Cleared sources only; CC BY-SA attribution on Janitor everywhere; written permission for Spotlight authors.
- **Content review for ads.** No gore (Janitor pages 3+, Masque pages 13–24) and no profanity (Janitor page 2).
- **Truthful proofs.** Every clip is real output. Omit failed proofs rather than relabel them; document edits in the edit record.
- **Name confusion.** The adjacent product "SceneWrite" exists; settle the name before large brand spend.
- **Readiness.** Growth that outruns reliability burns trust. Paid traffic waits for the launch gates.
